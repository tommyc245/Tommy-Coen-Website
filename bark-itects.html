Bark-itects

Wireless Alert System for a Diabetic Alert Dog — Duke University

Poster photo — add to images/DukeCoursework/
My Role

4-person team. Did all CAD for the project and led assembly and iteration of the sensor/bringsel subsystem.

Bark-itects is a wireless alert system built for a diabetic alert service dog. The dog wears an augmented bringsel — the pressure sensor the dog bites down on — with a sewn biothane exterior (the client suggested biothane; I specified and integrated it into the assembly). The handler wears a bracelet that vibrates via a motor mounted in the enclosure wall. I CAD'd and 3D printed both enclosures.

The two sides communicate over ESP-NOW using two Seeed XIAO ESP32C3 boards, with no phone or Wi-Fi dependency. Both interfaces run on a 3.7V 380mAh battery (electrical subsystem — not mine).

We went through 4 design revisions to tune the actuation threshold, solving two failure modes in sequence: the sensor not triggering on a real bite, and the sensor triggering too easily from routine dog movement.

Results vs. Requirements

  • Weight: 41g vs. <317g target — pass
  • Transmission reliability: 91.67% over 100 trials
  • Battery life: 6.33 hr vs. 9 hr target — did not meet
  • Water resistance (IPX5-6): untested
  • Bite resistance: ~1 year, estimated from client experience
